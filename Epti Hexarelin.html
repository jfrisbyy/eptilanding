01 · Quick Overview

Quick Overview.

Hexarelin is a synthetic hexapeptide and the most potent member of the GHRP [1] (Growth Hormone Releasing Peptide) family. Unlike Ipamorelin or GHRP-2, hexarelin has a unique dual mechanism: it stimulates GH release both by binding to the ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a) in the pituitary and by directly antagonising somatostatin, the hormone that suppresses GH release. [1] This makes it the most powerful GH secretagogue in its class, but also the one with the most pronounced side effects.

A notable and clinically studied secondary property is hexarelin's direct cardioprotective effect. It binds to CD36 receptors in cardiac tissue independently of GH [5], making it one of the few peptides with documented direct cardiac benefits. [2]

Who it is for:

  • Experienced users seeking maximum GH pulse amplitude.
  • Those with specific interest in cardioprotective peptide research.
  • Short-term use for accelerated recovery or body recomposition.

Who it is NOT for:

  • Beginners (Ipamorelin is a safer starting point).
  • Those prone to cortisol or prolactin sensitivity.
  • Tested athletes (banned by WADA [12]).

02 · The Protocol & Usage Guide

The Protocol & Usage Guide.

Before You Start: The Checklist

  • Baseline Bloodwork: IGF-1, cortisol (AM), prolactin, fasting glucose.
  • Supplies: Insulin syringes (31G, 5/16"), bacteriostatic water, alcohol swabs.
  • Reconstitution: Add 2 mL bacteriostatic water to a 2 mg vial = 1000 mcg/mL.

Standard Dosing Protocols

Experience LevelDoseFrequencyNotes
Beginner100 mcg1-2x/dayStart here to assess cortisol/prolactin response. community
Intermediate200 mcg2x/dayMost common community protocol. community
Advanced200 mcg3x/dayDiminishing returns above 200 mcg per injection. community

Note: Hexarelin exhibits significant desensitisation (tachyphylaxis) within 4-8 weeks [10] of continuous use. Cycling is essential. well-established [3]

Injection Timing & Fasting

  • Inject on an empty stomach (2 hours fasted) for maximum GH pulse.
  • Avoid eating for 30-60 minutes post-injection.
  • Best times: upon waking, pre-workout, or before bed.

Cycle Length

  • Standard Cycle: 4 to 8 weeks maximum due to rapid desensitisation. community
  • Time Off: Equal to or greater than cycle length (4-8 weeks off). community

Missed Dose Protocol

Skip the missed dose and resume the next scheduled injection. Do not double dose.

03 · Nutritional Support & Recommended Supplements

Nutritional Support & Recommended Supplements.

Macronutrient Alignment

  • Carbohydrates: Minimise around injection windows to prevent insulin blunting of GH pulse.
  • Protein: 1.6-2.2 g/kg body weight to support IGF-1-driven anabolism.

Micronutrients & Supplements

  • Zinc: 15-30 mg/day. Supports pituitary function and GH synthesis.
  • Magnesium Glycinate: 200-400 mg/day. Supports sleep quality and cortisol regulation.
  • Vitamin D3: 2000-5000 IU/day. Correlates with optimal IGF-1 levels.
  • Phosphatidylserine: 400-800 mg/day. May blunt the cortisol spike associated with hexarelin use. community

Safety, Interactions & Side Effect Management.

Contraindications

  • Absolute: Active malignancy, history of cancer, known hypersensitivity.
  • Relative: Pre-existing elevated cortisol or prolactin, history of prolactinoma.

Red Flags

Discontinue and consult a physician if:

  • Prolactin >25 ng/mL (men) or >30 ng/mL (women).
  • AM cortisol consistently elevated above the normal reference range.
  • Galactorrhoea (nipple discharge) in men.

Pregnancy, Lactation & Fertility

  • Pregnancy: Contraindicated. No human data; GH axis manipulation not recommended.
  • Lactation: Unknown; use not recommended.
  • Fertility: Elevated prolactin can suppress LH/FSH and impair fertility. Monitor if using long-term.

10 · Deep Dive (For Advanced Researchers)

Deep Dive (For Advanced Researchers).

Mechanism of Action

Hexarelin is a synthetic hexapeptide analogue of Met-enkephalin. It exerts its GH-releasing effects through two distinct mechanisms: (1) binding to the ghrelin receptor (GHSR-1a) in the anterior pituitary and hypothalamus, stimulating GH synthesis and release; and (2) directly antagonising somatostatin, the inhibitory hormone that suppresses GH release. [1] This dual action is what makes hexarelin uniquely potent compared to other GHRPs that act solely via GHSR-1a.

Additionally, hexarelin binds to CD36 (scavenger receptor class B) in cardiac and vascular tissue, independent of the GH axis. This interaction has been shown to exert direct cardioprotective effects, including protection against ischaemia-reperfusion injury and improvement in cardiac function. [2]

Clinical Trial Data

StudyDesignKey Findings
Ghigo 1994 [1]Phase 1 (n=30)Hexarelin produced dose-dependent GH release; 2 mcg/kg was the maximally effective dose.
Bisi 1999 [2]Human cardiac studyHexarelin improved left ventricular function in patients with GH deficiency; effects were partially GH-independent.
Laron 2004 [3]Desensitisation studyContinuous hexarelin administration led to significant GH axis desensitisation within 4 weeks; pulsatile dosing preserved responsiveness.
Imbimbo 1994 [9]Phase 1 RCT (n=18)IV hexarelin produced dose-dependent GH release; 2 mcg/kg was the maximally effective dose in healthy adults.
Rahim 1998 [10]6-month open-label (n=10)GH response attenuated after 16 weeks of daily dosing, supporting intermittent cycling protocols.
Mao 2014 [11]Narrative reviewSummarised cardioprotective effects of hexarelin including anti-fibrotic and anti-ischaemic properties via CD36 binding.

Active Metabolites

Hexarelin is metabolised by proteases into smaller inactive peptide fragments. No pharmacologically active metabolites have been identified.

Open Questions

  • Long-term cardiac outcomes: The CD36-mediated cardioprotective effects are well-documented in animal models but have not been studied in long-term human trials.
  • Optimal cycling strategy: The ideal on/off cycle ratio to preserve GHSR-1a sensitivity has not been formally studied in humans.
11 · Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ).

1. How does hexarelin compare to Ipamorelin? Hexarelin produces a much larger GH pulse but also significantly elevates cortisol and prolactin [9], which Ipamorelin does not. Hexarelin is better suited for short, intensive cycles; Ipamorelin is better for long-term, low-side-effect protocols.

2. Why does hexarelin stop working after a few weeks? Hexarelin causes rapid desensitisation (tachyphylaxis) of the GHSR-1a receptor. Cycling off for an equal period to your on-cycle allows receptor sensitivity to recover.

3. Does hexarelin need to be injected on an empty stomach? Yes. Insulin and somatostatin both blunt the GH pulse. Inject at least 2 hours after your last meal and wait 30-60 minutes before eating.

4. What is the cardioprotective effect of hexarelin? Hexarelin binds to CD36 receptors in cardiac tissue, independent of its GH-releasing effects. This has been shown to protect against ischaemia-reperfusion injury and improve cardiac function in clinical studies.

5. Can hexarelin cause gynecomastia? Hexarelin elevates prolactin, which in high concentrations can contribute to gynecomastia. Monitoring prolactin levels is recommended, and cabergoline can be used to manage elevated prolactin if needed.

6. What is the maximum effective dose of hexarelin? Research suggests 2 mcg/kg body weight is the maximally effective dose. Doses above this threshold do not produce proportionally greater GH release but do increase side effects.

7. Is hexarelin detectable in drug testing? Yes. Hexarelin is banned by WADA and is detectable in urine and blood samples. Tested athletes should not use it.
