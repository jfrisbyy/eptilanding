01 · Quick Overview

Quick Overview.

SS-31 (also known as Elamipretide, Bendavia, or Forzinity) is a synthetic tetrapeptide (four amino acids) specifically designed to target and repair mitochondria. While MOTS-c acts like the gas pedal for your cellular engines, SS-31 acts like the mechanic that fixes the engine itself.[1]

Inside your mitochondria, there is a specialized lipid (fat) called cardiolipin. It acts like the oil that keeps the engine running smoothly and maintains the structure of the inner mitochondrial membrane. As you age, or if you suffer from a chronic illness, cardiolipin becomes oxidized and damaged. The engine starts sputtering, leaking toxic exhaust (reactive oxygen species, or ROS) into your body. SS-31 dives directly into the mitochondria, binds to the cardiolipin, repairs the membrane, and stops the toxic exhaust, allowing the engine to produce clean energy (ATP) again.[2]

  • Primary Use Case: Repairing mitochondrial dysfunction, reversing chronic fatigue, and treating age-related energy decline.
  • Mechanism: Selectively binds to cardiolipin on the inner mitochondrial membrane, stabilizing its structure and restoring the efficiency of the electron transport chain.[2][3]
  • Who it is for: Older individuals, those recovering from chronic illness (Lyme, Long COVID, mold toxicity), or anyone suffering from severe, unexplained fatigue.
  • Who it is NOT for: Young, healthy individuals with perfectly functioning mitochondria (you will likely feel absolutely nothing from taking it).

10 · Deep Dive (For Advanced Researchers)

Deep Dive (For Advanced Researchers).

confidence_tier: well-established

Mechanism of Action

SS-31 (D-Arg-dimethylTyr-Lys-Phe-NH2) is a cell-permeable, mitochondria-targeted tetrapeptide.

  1. Cardiolipin Binding: SS-31 selectively targets and binds to cardiolipin (CL), a unique phospholipid found almost exclusively in the inner mitochondrial membrane (IMM). CL is essential for the optimal function of numerous enzymes involved in mitochondrial energy metabolism, including the electron transport chain (ETC) complexes.[2]
  2. Structural Stabilization: By binding to CL, SS-31 stabilizes the IMM structure, preventing the leakage of electrons from the ETC. This prevents the formation of reactive oxygen species (ROS) at the source, rather than just scavenging them after they are formed (which is how traditional antioxidants like Vitamin C work).[3]
  3. Cytochrome c Optimization: SS-31 protects cytochrome c (a key protein in the ETC) from peroxidase activity, ensuring efficient electron transfer and maximizing ATP production, even under ischemic or highly stressed conditions.[4]

Clinical Trial Summary

  • Barth Syndrome: In September 2025, the US FDA granted accelerated approval to Elamipretide (under the brand name Forzinity) as the first treatment for Barth syndrome, a rare genetic disorder characterized by abnormal cardiolipin remodeling and severe mitochondrial dysfunction.[5]
  • Primary Mitochondrial Myopathy (PMM): While earlier Phase 3 trials (MMPOWER-3) for PMM did not meet their primary endpoints for improving the 6-minute walk test, post-hoc analyses and ongoing studies continue to explore its efficacy in specific mitochondrial disease subsets.[6]
  • Heart Failure: Clinical trials have demonstrated that a single infusion of Elamipretide is safe and well-tolerated in patients with heart failure, resulting in favorable changes in left ventricular volumes.[7]
11 · Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ).

confidence_tier: community

Q: I'm a healthy 25-year-old athlete. Will SS-31 give me more energy? A: Probably not. If your mitochondria are already healthy and your cardiolipin is intact, SS-31 has nothing to "fix." It is not a stimulant. It is a repair tool for damaged or aging mitochondria.

Q: Why is it called SS-31, Elamipretide, and Bendavia? Are they the same thing? A: Yes, they are the exact same molecule. SS-31 is the original research name (named after the inventors Szeto and Schiller). Elamipretide is the generic pharmaceutical name. Bendavia and Forzinity are brand names.

Q: Can I take it orally? A: No. As a peptide, it will be destroyed by your stomach acid. It must be injected subcutaneously.
