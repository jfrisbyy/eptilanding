GLP-1 receptor agonists are the most effective pharmacological weight-loss tools ever developed. Semaglutide (Ozempic, Wegovy) produces 15–18% mean weight loss over 68 weeks. Tirzepatide (Mounjaro, Zepbound), which activates both GLP-1 and GIP receptors, achieves 22% over 72 weeks. Retatrutide — a triple agonist hitting GLP-1, GIP, and glucagon — has shown 24% at 48 weeks in Phase II and is the current frontier.

Frequently asked

Common questions.

Which GLP-1 produces the most weight loss?

Retatrutide has produced the largest weight loss in clinical trials (~24% at 48 weeks in Phase II), followed by tirzepatide (~22% at 72 weeks). Semaglutide produces ~15–18% over 68 weeks. Retatrutide is not yet FDA-approved.

Is tirzepatide better than semaglutide?

In head-to-head trials (SURMOUNT-5), tirzepatide produced significantly greater weight loss than semaglutide. However, side-effect profiles, costs, and availability differ. Choose based on goals, tolerance, and access.

Do I need to keep taking GLP-1s forever?

Discontinuation studies show most patients regain a significant portion of lost weight within 1–2 years off-drug. GLP-1s are typically managed as long-term metabolic therapy, similar to blood-pressure medication.

Can I switch between GLP-1s?

Yes — many users switch from semaglutide to tirzepatide for stronger weight loss, or downward when supply is constrained. Switching requires a brief washout and re-titration to manage GI side effects.
